How the Math Works

Each lane's carrier cost is miles × your RPM basis × (1 + contingency) — what you expect to pay the truck, before fuel. The fuel surcharge is a straight $/mile passthrough on both the cost and the sell side, so it never affects margin. Your sell linehaul is computed with margin on revenue, not markup on cost (carrier cost ÷ (1 − margin)) — the industry-standard way to hit a true target margin percentage. A minimum linehaul charge floors short lanes so fixed costs stay covered even when the RPM math alone would price too low.

Flags: Short Haul marks lanes under 250 miles, where per-mile economics break down. Floor Applied means the minimum charge kicked in — your margin on that lane will run above target, which is expected. Do Not Bid means the incumbent's rate sits below your cost floor — you cannot win it profitably as priced. Margin Squeeze means you can beat the incumbent, just not at your full target margin, and shows the best margin still achievable.

Manual-miles mode has no limit. Supply a miles column and price as many bids as you want. The optional Google road-mile lookup is convenient for unknown lanes but shares a site-wide daily quota with every other tool that uses it, so it's capped at 6 submissions per hour per connection — leave miles blank only for the lanes you actually need looked up to make the most of that allowance. Separately, every submission on this page — manual or Google — is capped at 30 per hour per connection as a general safeguard against automated abuse; a real person pricing a bid, tweaking assumptions, and re-running it will never come close to that number.
